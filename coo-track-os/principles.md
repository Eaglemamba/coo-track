# Operating Principles

These are non-negotiable. They govern how I operate, lead, and develop. Review quarterly. Update only with deliberate intent.

---

## 1. Reflection Before Optimization

Operational leaders default to fixing. The instinct is useful — until it prevents understanding. Before optimizing a process, a team, or a relationship, understand what is actually happening first. Diagnosis before prescription.

## 2. The 50% Output Rule

Time saved through AI, automation, or efficiency gains flows to creation — not consumption. If a dual-AI workflow saves 9 days on deviation reports, those days go toward leadership development, strategic thinking, or content creation. Not email.

## 3. Articulation as Diagnosis

When I cannot explain something clearly, the problem is understanding — not communication. Inability to articulate reveals gaps in thinking. The Articulator system exists for this reason: structured feedback on my explanations exposes where my understanding is shallow.

## 4. Timing Over Argument

The right moment matters more than the right logic. I demonstrated this by waiting six months post-layoffs to surface AI tools I had been developing. A good idea at the wrong time fails. Read the organizational moment before making the case.

## 5. Low-Friction Capabilities Require High-Discipline Protocols

When tools reduce activation energy — when it becomes easy to generate, analyze, or automate — the risk is capability drift. Easy does not mean valuable. Explicit protocols prevent the system from doing more while achieving less.

## 6. Build Infrastructure, Not Tooling

AI adoption is not a set of tools bolted onto existing processes. It creates a fourth organizational capability — the Tooling Layer — alongside culture, practice, and accountability. Treat it as infrastructure. Build it to last. Document it for others.

## 7. Show the Process, Not Just the Conclusion

In leadership, in content, in this system: capture the failure, the adjustment, the thinking in motion. Polished summaries obscure the actual learning. The value is in how thinking evolves, not where it lands.

## 8. Develop Others by Releasing Work

Holding work that others should own is not excellence — it is a failure of development. Delegation is not about efficiency. It is about building the team's capability. Every task I hold that someone else could grow from is a missed leadership opportunity.

## 9. Compliance Is Not Bureaucracy

In GMP environments, rigor protects patients. Regulatory discipline is not overhead — it is the operating standard. Leadership in regulated environments means holding quality and innovation simultaneously without treating them as trade-offs.

## 10. Cross-Functional Fluency Is the COO Foundation

Operations expertise alone does not qualify a COO. The transition requires genuine fluency in Quality, Supply Chain, Commercial, Finance, and Regulatory. Not awareness — fluency. The kind that allows me to make judgment calls outside my home domain.
