# Mental Models

These frameworks guide my thinking. They are not theories — they are patterns I have tested in practice. Each one has a trigger condition: a situation where applying the model improves my decision-making.

---

## 1. Three-Question Reflection

**The model**: After any significant experience, ask three questions:
1. "What patterns in my reasoning?"
2. "Where did I get stuck?"
3. "What thinking habits helped or hurt?"

**When to apply**: After meetings, difficult conversations, project milestones, content creation sessions, and at the end of every daily check-in.

**Why it matters**: Most reflection is narrative — "here's what happened." These questions force structural analysis of thinking itself. The goal is not to evaluate outcomes but to examine the reasoning process.

---

## 2. The 50% Output Rule

**The model**: When AI or automation saves time, at least 50% of that time flows to creation — not consumption, not more meetings, not more email.

**When to apply**: Every time a workflow improvement lands. If the dual-AI deviation system saves 9 days, 4.5 of those days go to leadership development, strategic work, or content creation.

**Why it matters**: Efficiency gains without output discipline just create faster busy-work. The rule prevents time savings from being absorbed by organizational noise.

---

## 3. Forcing Function Principle

**The model**: Rituals and structured protocols create extraction discipline for insights. Without forcing functions, operational momentum prevents reflection.

**When to apply**: This entire operating system is a forcing function. The daily 5-minute check-in forces a pause. The weekly review forces pattern recognition. The quarterly review forces strategic assessment.

**Why it matters**: In operations, the urgent always crowds out the important. Forcing functions ensure the important gets its time — not because it feels urgent, but because the ritual demands it.

---

## 4. Mid-Process Awareness

**The model**: Insights emerge during conversation and work, not just at the end. Capture them as they surface rather than waiting for a tidy conclusion.

**When to apply**: During any extended thinking session — writing, stakeholder conversations, project planning. When something clicks mid-process, stop and record it immediately.

**Why it matters**: The best insights are often fleeting. If you wait until the end of a session to summarize, you lose the thinking in motion. This model is why the content pipeline exists — to catch publishable insights as they surface.

---

## 5. Articulation as Diagnosis

**The model**: Inability to explain something clearly reveals understanding gaps, not communication problems.

**When to apply**: When I struggle to explain a decision, a strategy, or a concept — to my team, my leadership, or in content. The Articulator system operationalizes this.

**Why it matters**: Leaders who cannot articulate their reasoning cannot align others. In a COO role, every decision must be communicable across functions. Unclear articulation means unclear thinking.

---

## 6. Explore vs. Execute Mode

**The model**: Two distinct modes of working with AI and with problems. In Explore mode, question engineering dominates — ask better questions, probe assumptions, map the territory. In Execute mode, prompt engineering dominates — clear instructions, specific outputs, defined quality standards.

**When to apply**: At the start of any task, explicitly choose: am I exploring or executing? The failure mode is exploring when you should be executing (analysis paralysis) or executing when you should be exploring (premature optimization).

**Why it matters**: Conflating the two modes wastes time and produces poor results. Strategic thinking requires exploration. Operational delivery requires execution. Know which mode you are in.

---

## 7. Low-Friction Capabilities Require High-Discipline Protocols

**The model**: When tools reduce activation energy, explicit protocols prevent capability drift. Easy does not mean valuable.

**When to apply**: Every time a new AI capability is deployed. When the dual-AI deviation system went live, the protocol defined exactly what "good" looks like — preventing the tool from producing more output without more value.

**Why it matters**: This is the central tension of AI adoption in operations. The tools make everything easier. Without discipline protocols, teams generate more while achieving less.

---

## 8. Constraint Layering Framework

**The model**: Systematically identify three layers of constraints before proposing solutions:
1. **Platform constraints** — What the technology or system cannot do
2. **Team conventions** — What the team has agreed to do (or not do)
3. **Non-negotiable requirements** — Regulatory, compliance, safety requirements that cannot be traded away

**When to apply**: Before any significant process change, technology adoption, or organizational proposal. Especially critical in GMP environments where non-negotiable requirements are extensive.

**Why it matters**: Most failed initiatives fail because they ignored a constraint layer. In pharma, the non-negotiable layer (GMP, regulatory) is larger than in most industries. Mapping constraints first prevents wasted effort.

---

## 9. Timing Over Argument

**The model**: The right moment matters more than the right logic. A good idea at the wrong time fails.

**When to apply**: Before any significant proposal, change initiative, or difficult conversation. Ask: "Is this the right time for this message? What is the organizational moment?"

**Evidence**: I waited six months post-layoffs to surface AI tools I had been developing. The timing made the difference between "David is replacing people with AI" and "David has been building tools that help us do more with what we have."

**Why it matters**: Operations leaders often underweight timing because they focus on logic and data. COOs cannot afford that. Organizational readiness determines whether good ideas succeed or fail.

---

## 10. Tooling Layer Principle

**The model**: AI adoption creates a fourth organizational capability beyond culture, practice, and accountability. Build it as infrastructure, not tooling.

**When to apply**: When designing any AI workflow, process, or system. The question is not "what tool solves this?" but "what organizational capability are we building?"

**Why it matters**: Tools are disposable. Infrastructure persists. The Builder Triad (Validation Designers, Pattern Analysts, Workflow Architects) represents the human side of this infrastructure. See `frameworks/tooling_layer.md` for the full framework.
